EMA BACKTESTER

Enter any ticker or contract address, pick a timeframe and an EMA (20, 50, 100 or 200), set your dollars, and see what buying and selling on the signals would have done compared to just holding. Further down, the Rule Matrix tests every EMA on daily, weekly and monthly candles back up to 15 years, and the Strategy Maker shows how a rule could play out over the next 90, 180 or 365 days.

1 · Token & Data

In TradingView, open your chart, click the layout menu (top right) and choose "Export chart data". Upload that file here and pick the same timeframe label as the chart. A header row with a "close" column is detected automatically. Dates can be ISO dates or Unix timestamps.
Where the data comes from: tickers try Binance, Bybit, OKX, Coinbase, then CoinGecko. Contract addresses (0x... or a Solana mint) use GeckoTerminal, which picks the deepest-liquidity pool for that token on the network you choose. TradingView has no public data feed, so use its CSV export above, or load its live chart below the results for a side-by-side look.

2 · Signal

Signal strength (0 to 100): above the EMA is a buy, below is a sell. The signal is strongest on a fresh cross, and stronger still when price spent a long time on the other side first (a bear market before a cross up, a bull market before a cross down). "Prolonged" is how many EMA lengths the earlier stretch lasted, and "fresh window" is how quickly the cross fades. The last two boxes let the backtest act only on strong signals, for example buy at 85 or higher and sell at 15 or lower.

Rule Matrix · every EMA on daily, weekly and monthly, back to 15 years

Buy when a candle closes above the EMA, sell when one closes below it. This runs the 20, 50, 100 and 200 EMA (and any you add) on daily, weekly and monthly candles side by side against buy-and-hold. It uses the ticker, data source and network from section 1 and your starting capital and fee from section 3.
LOOK BACK
A 50 EMA on weekly candles is 50 weeks. A signal only counts when a candle closes on the other side, so wicks trigger nothing. The EMA is warmed up on all the history before your start date. Monthly 100 and 200 EMAs need about 8 and 17 years of history, so they only show up when the coin has that much.

Strategy Maker · the next 90, 180 or 365 days

Nobody can predict price, so this does not try to. It builds hundreds or thousands of possible futures by reshuffling real days from the coin's own past (in 20-day blocks, so crashes and rallies come in clumps like they really do), then runs the chosen rule through every one. You see the spread of outcomes, where the rule stands today, and the price that would flip it.

Ask the EMA Desk (AI) members only

Ask in plain words, for example: Which rule would you pick for Bitcoin and why? Is the weekly 50 EMA about to flip? Why did the 200 EMA lag so much? It reads the Rule Matrix, the Strategy Maker and the current signal you have on screen. Run the Rule Matrix first. Education only, not financial advice.
Read this before you trade. The signal strength score is a rule-of-thumb built from how fresh an EMA cross is and how long price stayed on the other side first. It has not been proven to predict anything, so test it with the backtest filters before leaning on it. This is an educational tool, not financial advice. Backtests use hindsight and do not predict future results. Signals execute at the close of the candle that triggered them, with no slippage, spreads or taxes. Only the fee you enter is modeled. The strategy is long-only spot: it holds the token or cash, with no leverage or shorting. EMA strategies often whipsaw in sideways markets, and past cycles may not repeat.
