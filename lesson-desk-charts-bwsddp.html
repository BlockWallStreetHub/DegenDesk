Reading Charts with Desk Charts

Chart any coin against the dollar, or against any other coin, and learn to see which one is actually winning.

1. Two coins, one chart

A normal chart answers "what is AVAX worth in dollars?" A cross pair answers "what is AVAX worth in ETH?" It is the AVAX price divided by the ETH price, candle by candle. If the line rises, AVAX is beating ETH. If it falls, you would have been better off holding ETH, even when both are rising in dollars.

Try it: pick AVAX in the first box and ETH in the second, or use the quick pair chips. Press ⇆ to flip it to ETH / AVAX.

2. The controls

  • Timeframes: 5m to 1W. Short for timing, long for the big picture.
  • Scroll to zoom, drag to move, drag the right-hand price scale to stretch it, double-click it to auto-fit.
  • Log scale shows percentage moves evenly; use it on long histories.
  • Indicators: EMA 20/50/100/200, SMA 50/200, Bollinger Bands, RSI, MACD and Volume.
  • Chart types: candles, hollow, Heikin Ashi, bars, line and area.

3. Drawing tools

  • Trend line: click two points along a series of highs or lows.
  • Horizontal line: mark a level price keeps reacting to.
  • Fib retracement: click the swing low then the swing high to see common pullback zones.
  • Measure: shows the price change, percent, bars and time between two points.

Click a drawing to select it, drag it to move it, press Delete to remove it. Drawings are saved per pair in your browser.

4. The Rotation board

The right panel ranks coins over 24 hours, 7 days and 30 days relative to your quote coin. Set the second box to ETH and green means the coin beat ETH. Click a coin to load its chart.

5. Divergence and the AI Analyst

Divergence is when price and an indicator disagree. Desk Charts finds it for you and draws it on the chart and on the RSI pane.

  • Bullish divergence: price makes a lower low but RSI makes a higher low. Selling is losing strength, so a bounce may be near.
  • Bearish divergence: price makes a higher high but RSI makes a lower high. Buying is losing strength, so a pullback may be near.
  • Hidden bullish: price makes a higher low but RSI makes a lower low. It points to an uptrend continuing.
  • Hidden bearish: price makes a lower high but RSI makes a higher high. It points to a downtrend continuing.

Solid lines are regular divergences and dashed lines are hidden ones. They are confirmed a few bars after the swing forms, so they show up late on purpose. Open the AI Analyst tab for a plain-English read, a factor checklist, support and resistance levels, and a scan of your watchlist for fresh divergences.

6. Good habits

  • Look at the cross pair as well as the dollar chart before you rotate from one coin into another.
  • Use more than one timeframe, and wait for a close before trusting a level break.
  • Indicators describe the past. They are not predictions.
