How this works
  • Each purchase uses the real Bitcoin price from that day. Nothing is averaged or smoothed.
  • Your set-aside = monthly sales × profit margin × your share (or sales × share, or a fixed dollar amount). Weekly and bi-weekly buys split that monthly amount evenly.
  • The fee is taken out of each purchase, so a 0.5% fee on $1,000 buys $995 of Bitcoin.
  • The savings line puts the same dollars on the same dates into an account earning your chosen rate, compounded daily.
  • Annualized return is money-weighted, so it accounts for when each dollar went in.
What this does not include
  • Taxes. Selling or spending Bitcoin can trigger tax, and how a business holds it affects your books. Talk to a CPA.
  • Storage and security costs, network fees, or exchange spreads beyond the fee you entered.
  • The future. Bitcoin has had deep drops, including falls of more than 70%. This shows the past, not a forecast.
  • Cash you may need. Many owners only use money they will not need for operations.
