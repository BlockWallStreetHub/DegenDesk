Put Spreads & the Derive Simulator

Learn to bet on a drop with a fixed, known risk, then test any Derive trade before you place it.

1. The idea

Buying a put on its own is expensive when you only expect a measured drop. A long put spread cuts the cost: you buy a put at a higher strike and sell a put at a lower strike, same expiry. The put you sold pays for part of the one you bought.

Example: BTC is near $73,200. Buy the $72,500 put, sell the $65,000 put, 20 BTC, expiring end of December.

  • Max loss = what you paid (the debit). It happens if BTC finishes above $72,500.
  • Max profit = strike width ($7,500) minus the debit, times size. It happens at or below $65,000.
  • Breakeven = long strike minus the debit per BTC. With a debit of about $1,415, that is about $71,100.

2. Why choose a spread

  • Cheaper than a single put, and you know the worst case before you click.
  • Works best when volatility is low (options are cheap) and you expect a move to a level, not a crash to zero.
  • The trade-off: profit stops at the lower strike. If BTC falls through it, you do not keep gaining.

3. Use the Put Spread Calculator

Open the calculator, type spot, both strikes, the two option prices from Derive and your size. Read four numbers: net debit, max loss, max profit, breakeven. Then use the price ladder to see your profit at your take-profit target.

4. Use the Derive Trade Simulator

The simulator builds any trade: calls, puts, spreads, straddles, iron condors, collars and perps. Three sliders matter most:

  • Days passed: shows how time decay changes the value before expiry.
  • BTC price: where you think the market goes.
  • IV change: options get pricier when fear rises, and cheaper when it fades.

Prices come from a model. Always replace them with the real quote on Derive, and give each leg its own IV, because lower strikes usually trade at a higher IV.

5. Rules for the Desk

  • Never risk money you cannot afford to lose; size so the max loss is a small slice of your account.
  • Check the fee schedule and the real bid and ask before trading.
  • A simulator is practice, not a promise.
